---
title: "Browsing for you"
description: "Ask in the chat for something done on a website. Niles reads the pages itself, and every step that changes something waits for your Allow."
---

Niles has a browser of its own. Ask in the chat for something done on a
website, such as "put a 2 m USB-C cable in my basket on shop.example", and
Niles opens the site, reads its pages and works out the steps. Opening and
reading a page needs nothing from you. Each step that changes something
waits on a card until you allow it.

To read a public page, Niles doesn't need its browser. The browser is for
pages that need clicks, a form or a login.

## What to ask

Ask in the app's chat. Browsing happens there and nowhere else:

- Asked by voice, Niles tells you to ask in the app, where the cards are.
- Automations and other background jobs can't browse.
- Each person has a browsing session of their own. Two of your
  conversations share yours, and its page.

Name the site and say what you want done there, such as "book the 18:00
delivery slot on shop.example". Niles tells you when it needs a login
first.

## The cards

Every click, every bit of typing, every choice from a list and every form
Niles submits waits on a card under its reply. The card asks the step, such
as **Click "Add to basket"?**, with the site under the question. It shows a
picture of the page with what Niles would act on outlined, the page's
address, the element, the text or the option when there is one, and the
model's reason for the step.

- **Allow once** does that one step, exactly as the card shows it. Niles
  then carries on by itself and reads the page again.
- **Deny** does nothing, and Niles stops there.

These cards have no **Allow for this conversation**, and nothing answers
them by voice. A notification about one offers **Review**, which opens the
card, instead of **Approve**. Only you see your cards.

Niles checks the page again when you press **Allow once**. If the page
changed after the card was made, the step lapses and nothing is done. When
what the card was for is still on the page, a new card shows it as the page
is now. Otherwise Niles stops, and you ask again. A card also lapses when
nobody answers it within an hour, and when Niles or its browser restarts.

A purchase takes several cards: adding to the basket, going to the
checkout, signing in, perhaps a code, a delivery time and the order itself.
Niles follows links by opening their addresses, which needs no card.

## Placing an order

The step that places an order or pays gets a card of its own, **Place this
order on shop.example?**, with the order's **Total** and **Delivery
address** beside the picture. Niles reads both from the page, never from
the model. When it can't find one, the card says "Niles couldn't find it
on the page", and the picture shows what the page says.

Niles knows an order step by the button's label, such as **Place order**,
**Pay now** or **Buy now**, and by the checkout around it. On a checkout
page most buttons get the order card, so you may see it before the last
step too. If the total or the address changes before you press **Allow
once**, the step lapses and a new card shows the new ones.

## Paying

Niles pays only with what your account on the site has saved, such as a
saved card. It never asks for card details, keeps them or types them.

A page that asks for card details stops the run, and Niles tells you the
site wants them. Save a card on your account on the site yourself, or
finish that order on the site. Niles also refuses to type anything that
reads as a card number.

If your bank wants to approve the payment (3-D Secure), approve it in the
bank's app, then tell Niles to carry on. A bank that wants a code typed on
the page can't be answered through Niles. Finish that order on the site.

## Logins

When a site needs you signed in and Niles has no login for it, the chat
shows "Shop wants a login", Shop being the name Niles gave the site, with
**Add login**. The form opens with the site filled in. The username and
password you type there go straight to Niles, never into the chat or to
the model.

You can also add a login before you need it, under **My profile →
Logins**. **Add login** asks for a label, the site's address, the username
and the password. A site that signs in or checks out on another address
needs that address too. Each login in the list has three buttons:

- **Edit** shows the password as saved. Leave it empty to keep it.
- **Sign out** forgets what the site stored in Niles's browser for that
  login, and keeps the login.
- **Remove** deletes the login and what the site stored.

Only you see your logins, and nobody can read a password back. Saved
logins need Niles's database and its secrets key.

### Signing in

Niles signs in on a card, **Sign in to Shop?**, which shows the site and
the login's label, never the username. When you allow it, Niles fills in
the form itself, only on that login's own sites, and the model learns only
whether it worked. A signed-in session stays on the login's sites. To open
another site, Niles starts a new session that isn't signed in.

After a sign-in, Niles keeps what the site stored in its browser, its
cookies and storage, encrypted, so the next visit doesn't ask for a code
each time. The **Signed in** badge on the Logins page shows Niles has
them. They go 30 days after the login was last used, and when you press
**Sign out** or **Remove**.

## Codes and CAPTCHAs

When a site sends you a code by text message, mail or an authenticator
app, the chat shows "Shop sent you a code" with a field. Type the code
there. It goes to the page and nowhere else, not to the chat and not to
the model. A wrong code gets a new card. A code card lapses after 15
minutes.

When a page wants you to show you aren't a robot (a CAPTCHA), Niles stops
and tells you the site wants a person. Niles doesn't solve CAPTCHAs, and
you can't take over its browser.

## Sites that say no

Niles doesn't hide that its browser is automated, so a site that refuses
automated browsers refuses Niles too. Nothing in Niles changes that.

A site may also ask your account to verify itself when Niles signs in for
the first time, by mail or with a code, as it would for any new browser.
What Niles keeps after a sign-in makes that rarer.

A signed-in session stays on its login's sites. If a shop sends you to
another site to pay, add that site's address to the shop's login.

## Setting it up

Browsing needs Niles's browser, a headless Chromium in a container of its
own, and Niles's database, which keeps the cards with their conversation.

- On Kubernetes, add `components: [../../components/browser]` to your
  overlay's `kustomization.yaml`. See
  [Kubernetes](/docs/self-hosting/kubernetes#browsing).
- With Docker Compose, start it with
  `docker compose -f compose.yaml -f browser.yaml up -d`. See
  [Docker Compose](/docs/self-hosting/docker-compose#browsing).

Niles looks for the browser by its name, `niles-browser`, and turns
browsing on by itself within a minute of the browser starting. There is
nothing to set in `niles.toml`. Until it finds one, Niles says browsing
isn't set up.

The browser reaches the internet only through Niles, which lets it open
public `https` sites and nothing on your home network or in your cluster.
On Kubernetes the component adds a NetworkPolicy as a second wall. It
holds only where the cluster's network plugin enforces NetworkPolicies:
Cilium and Calico do, and Flannel, Talos's default, doesn't.

## What Niles keeps

- Your logins, encrypted in Niles's database.
- What a site stored in Niles's browser after a sign-in, encrypted, until
  30 days after the login was last used.
- A card's picture of the page, in memory, until the card is answered or
  lapses.

The browser keeps nothing between sessions, and Niles restarts it once a
day when no session is open.
