---
title: "Approvals"
description: "Every mail, and every tool set to ask first, waits for your yes in the app."
---

Some things Niles never does on its own. It holds them, shows you exactly
what it is about to do, and waits for your yes. Two kinds of thing wait:

- **Every mail it writes.** Niles never sends a mail by itself, whoever
  asked and however.
- **A tool set to ask first.** A remote tool server, such as Linear, lists
  its tools on its panel under **Settings → Add-ons**. Each tool can be
  **On**, **Ask first** or **Off**. A tool with no setting is on. The
  badges **Reads only** and **Can delete** help you choose.

Niles's own tools (lights, timers, Saved and the rest) never ask.

When you say yes, Niles runs exactly the call it showed you, with the
same values. The model has no say in it after that.

## The card

A tool card is titled after what it would do, such as "Let Linear create
an issue?", and lists each value it would send. Long values fold behind
an arrow.

A mail card is titled **Send this email?**, says which account it is from,
and shows To, Cc, Subject and the whole message.

The buttons are:

- **Allow once**: do this one thing.
- **Allow for this conversation**: do it, and let this tool run without
  asking for the rest of this conversation. Only tool cards in the app
  have this. Mail never does, and nor does anything asked by voice.
- **Deny**: do nothing.

After you decide, the card says what happened: **Allowed. Done.**,
**Denied. Nothing was done.**, **Lapsed. Nothing was done.**, or that it
was allowed but did not work, and why.

"Allow for this conversation" lasts until Niles restarts.

## Where the cards are

- **In the chat**, under the reply that asked.
- **In Niles's panel**, on the **Approvals** tab (the shield). A badge
  shows how many wait. A link to `#/chat?panel=approvals` opens it.
- **On My profile → Approvals**: "What Niles would not do on its own: a
  mail to send, a tool set to Ask first. Only you see yours."

The panel and the profile show the same two lists. **Needs review** has
the cards still waiting, each folded to one line with **Details** to open
it, when it was asked and how long it waits, and **Show in the chat** when
it belongs to a conversation. **Approvals history** lists what you
decided, such as "Allowed once · 15:34" or "Denied by voice · 12 Oct", 30
at a time with **Show older**.

A link to `#/chat?approval=` followed by an approval's id opens that card
in its conversation, scrolled into view and outlined. One asked by voice
has no conversation, so the link opens it in **Approvals** instead. Every
notification about an approval links there.

Deciding in the panel is the same as pressing the button on the card in
the chat.

Only the person who asked sees an approval and can answer it. To anyone
else it does not exist.

## Answering by voice

When a tool asks first after a spoken request, Niles asks you out loud.
A plain "yes" or "no" to the same satellite decides it. Niles reads the
words itself; anything else leaves the question waiting. If Niles knew
the voice that asked, only that voice can answer. A spoken yes is
**Allow once**.

The same question also shows in the app and goes to your phone, so you
can answer it there instead.

Mail is never approved by voice. Ask for a mail by voice and Niles tells
you it is written and waiting in the app for you to check and send.

Typing "yes" in the chat does not approve a card. Use the buttons.

## How long it waits

| Asked | Waits |
|---|---|
| by voice | 5 minutes |
| in the app | 1 hour |

After that it lapses and nothing is done. A restart of Niles also lapses
everything still waiting.

A request that asks first, made where nobody is there to answer (by an
automation, say), fails at once rather than waiting.

## On your phone or computer

Each approval Niles holds for you is on your
[Notifications](/docs/using/notifications) page at once. It also goes to
every phone and computer where you turned notifications on, and only to
yours. A voice Niles did not recognise gets none.

It is sent at once when it was asked by voice, or by something running in
the background. Asked in the chat, it is sent only if it still waits a
minute later. Answer the card within the minute and no device is told.

It says the card's question and whose it is ("Send this email? · From your
Gmail, mark@gmail.com"), one line of what it would do, and how long it
waits ("Waiting until 19:42"). Tapping it opens the card.

On Android and on a computer it has buttons. Every notification has
**Deny**. **Approve** is **Allow once**, offered only when a spoken yes
could also allow it, which means a tool set to ask first. A mail has
**Review** instead, which opens the card, because a mail is read in full
before it is sent.

A button answers without opening Niles, as you, signed in on that device.
The notification then says what happened. If Niles can't be reached, or
that device is signed out, it says "Couldn't answer. Open Niles to answer
it." Tapping that opens the card.

An iPhone or iPad shows no buttons on notifications from a Home Screen
app. Tap the notification, then answer on the card.

Once it is answered, wherever that happened, or once it lapses, Niles
replaces the notification on your other devices with what became of it,
such as **Allowed. Done.** On Android and a computer the replacement
makes no sound.

## What the history keeps

The history keeps, for each decision, what the tool was, the card's
title, a short summary, what you decided, how and when. It never keeps
the values themselves. A value whose label looks like a password, a token
or a key shows as dots in the summary, and a mail's summary is only who it
was to and its subject, never the text.

Each person keeps their newest 500 entries. Without Niles's database
there is no history: "Niles has no database here, so it keeps no history
of what you decided."
