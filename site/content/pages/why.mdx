---
title: "Why Niles"
description: "Why Niles is built the way it is, what leaves the house and when, and whether it fits yours."
---

Niles is a home assistant you talk to. You say "Niles" to the satellite in
the room and ask for what you want, and the answer comes back from the same
satellite, usually in well under a second. It runs on a machine in your
house, with Docker Compose or Kubernetes, and keeps what it knows in your
own Postgres. This page explains the choices behind that.

## Speed first on the voice path

A spoken command never waits on something it doesn't need: a plugin, the
database, a second model or a cloud call. Audio streams in and the reply
streams out, and each add-on runs on its own thread, so a slow one holds up
nothing else.

## Tiers by trigger

Each step runs only when the one before it could not answer.

- **Fixed phrasings first.** Lights, blinds, scenes, music, the TV, timers,
  heating, the weather and the clock are matched by regex and carried out
  with no model at all. The target is about half a second end to end.
- **A fast model for the rest.** Anything else goes to a fast model
  (Cerebras by default) with only the tools that request is likely to need.
  You can say it your own way, and it takes a second or so.
- **The assistant for bigger jobs.** Making a rule, planning a trip,
  writing a podcast or reading a photo goes to Claude Code or ChatGPT. By
  voice it gets only the turns that need it. In the app's chat it answers
  whatever the fixed phrasings don't.

The model asks the device registry what is on through tool calls, instead
of searching a pile of text about the house. See
[the voice loop](/docs/architecture/voice-loop) for each step.

## Its own satellites and wake word

The satellite is a reSpeaker XVF3800 with a XIAO ESP32-S3, running Niles's
own firmware. A wake word model trained for "Niles" runs on the satellite
itself, so nothing leaves the room until the word is said. Then it streams
raw 16 kHz audio to Niles over TCP and plays the reply. The XMOS chip
cancels echo, so it hears you over music, and the room's Sonos turns down
while Niles listens and speaks.

Every satellite runs the same firmware and keeps no room state. Niles knows
which room one is in from its address, so a new satellite flashed with the
same firmware and given the old one's address takes its place. See [Satellites and hardware](/docs/setting-up/satellites).

## The assistant on your own subscription

The assistant is Claude Code or ChatGPT, signed in with the household's own
Claude or ChatGPT subscription. Both programs ship in the Niles image, and
Niles gives them its tools through its own MCP server, so it can switch a
light or read a calendar the same way the fast model does. There is no
Niles account, and no service of the authors' between you and the model.

## The house is described upstream

Rooms and devices are the names you give them in Zigbee2MQTT, as
`room/device`: `kitchen/ceiling`, `living_room/floor_lamp`. Niles keeps no
device database of its own, and no screen in the app creates a room. The app
changes values and presses switches. Name things well once and every new
device turns up in the right room. See
[Naming your devices](/docs/setting-up/naming).

## One house, several people

The house has one state: one set of devices, one lighting curve, one set of
scenes. Two people can't both own the curve.

People get personal references instead. "My calendar" and "wake me up" mean
the speaker's own calendar and alarm, found from the voice Niles recognised or
the person signed in to the app. A voice Niles doesn't know reaches nobody's
calendar, mail or notes. Voice prints stay in the house's own database, and
only people who enrolled are recognised.

## The model approves nothing

The model never decides an approval. When it wants to send a mail or use a
tool set to ask first, Niles holds the exact call and shows it to you. What
runs after your yes is what you saw. A mail is never sent without a press in
the app: never by voice, and no setting turns that off.

What a web page, a file, a tool result or an add-on says is information to
the model, never an instruction. A PDF that says "ignore your rules" is read,
not obeyed.

## Local first

What Niles keeps stays in your house, in your Postgres: settings, rules,
conversations, Saved items, voice prints and timers. Keys typed into the
app are sealed in that database with a key only your deployment holds.

These run on your own Niles and need no outside service: the fixed
phrasings, the lighting curve, recognising voices, speaking the replies
(Piper), rules and timers. With the internet down, wall switches, the
curve, rules and timers keep working. Voice needs the speech-to-text
service, unless you run an OpenAI-compatible one of your own.

There is no telemetry. Niles sends nothing about you or your use of it to
its authors or anyone else.

## What leaves the house, and when

| Service | What goes there | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speech to text (ElevenLabs Scribe, or any OpenAI-compatible server) | The audio of a command | After the wake word, never before |
| The fast model (Cerebras, or any OpenAI-compatible server) | The words of a command, with the context it needs | Only when no fixed phrasing caught it |
| Claude or ChatGPT | Chat messages, attached files, rules to work out, the background review | Only once you switch one on |
| The add-ons and accounts you connect | What each one needs to answer | While it is connected |
| Open-Meteo | The house's location | When Niles needs the weather |
| Apple, Google or Mozilla push | A notification for your phone | When Niles has something to tell you |
| GitHub | Signing in, add-on downloads and update checks | When someone signs in, and for installed add-ons |

The full list is on
[Security and privacy](/docs/using/security#what-leaves-the-house).

## Extending it

Built-in integrations (Sonos, LG TVs, tado°, calendars, Strava and the rest)
are crates in the repository, held to its tests. A remote MCP tool server,
such as Linear's, brings a service's own tools. For the rest there are
[add-ons](/add-ons): WebAssembly components installed from a GitHub release.

An add-on reaches only what its manifest declares and the household approved.
It gets no undeclared hosts, no other add-on's secrets, no database, no
conversations and no code in the app. That limits what an add-on can reach. It
does not make one trustworthy, so the approval screen says plainly what is
granted, and an update that asks for more is approved again.

## Is it a fit?

Niles is a good fit if:

- your devices are Zigbee, through Zigbee2MQTT, or LED strips on WLED, with
  a few Wi-Fi devices beside them;
- you want to talk to the house, and to be answered from the room you are
  in;
- you are comfortable running a container and a database, with Docker
  Compose or Kubernetes;
- the household speaks English.

It is not a fit yet if:

- you need hundreds of integrations, or Z-Wave or Matter;
- you want to describe the house in a UI, adding rooms and devices from
  setup screens.

If you need the widest device support, that is what Home Assistant is built
for.

Niles is also young. Its releases are 0.x, and breaking changes are
expected.
